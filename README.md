# Checking-Whether-AI-Search-Gets-Your-Business-Right
Compare AI search descriptions with current business facts, save customer-style questions and correct sources behind misleading answers.


An AI answer may name your business and still get an important fact wrong. It could describe a discontinued service or direct customers to an old location. Checking AI search accuracy means examining the description, rather than counting a mention as success. Begin with the facts a customer needs before contacting you. A repeatable review helps separate false details from missing information and turns findings into specific corrections.

**Set Out the Business Facts to Check**

Record the current business facts before you ask an assistant to describe the company. List the name, service area, contact information and a plain explanation of what you do. Compare those details against the pages your customers actually see. If your own website and listings disagree, there is already a discrepancy to fix.
Accuracy and visibility require separate checks. An absent mention means the business did not appear in that answer. A wrong mention means the answer misrepresented something. Record those findings separately so a flattering description does not hide an error and a single omission does not become a sweeping conclusion about the business.
Keep the review focused on practical effects. Which mistake would confuse a prospective customer or cause an unsuitable enquiry? An incorrect service description warrants attention even when the rest of the paragraph sounds positive. Treat current facts as the reference point, rather than judging an answer by its confidence or tone.

**Ask Customer-Style Questions and Save Answers**

Choose a small, consistent set of questions. Ask a direct question about the business and a question about the need it serves without naming the business. The first tests the description of a known company; the second reveals whether the company appears when a customer starts with a problem.
Ask the questions across the AI search services relevant to your audience. Responses can differ between runs and contexts, so one result is a snapshot. Preserve the full wording, including omissions and qualifications. A later summary may omit the exact sentence that needs checking.
Maintain a simple record for each test:
The precise question and the date of the check.
The AI service used and the complete answer returned.
Any pages cited and the specific fact that needs review.
Retain the same core questions for later comparisons. Log new questions separately when your services change, rather than replacing the whole set and losing the ability to compare results.

**Inspect the Pages Behind the Answer**

Read the cited links before assuming they support the answer. Verify whether each page describes your business, whether its information is current and whether it actually supports the statement beside it.
Record the page URL with the specific mismatch. That makes the correction task specific: a business description, service page or outside listing needs attention. If no citation appears, mark the source as unknown instead of inventing an explanation for the answer.
A citation offers a lead to inspect; it does not make every sentence in the answer reliable. Review the wording carefully and keep the source text separate from the assistant's interpretation.

**Correct Source Details, Then Recheck**

If an answer cites sources, open them and check whether they support the description. A linked page could be outdated, may refer to another business or may simply fail to support the particular statement. Document the difference between what the source says and what the answer claims.
Fix information you control first. Revise unclear service pages and inconsistent contact details, then review relevant business listings. If an outside page contains an error, request a factual correction from its owner. Make the wording specific enough that a visitor can understand the business without filling in gaps.
A correction cannot guarantee an immediate change in AI answers. Run the same checks again and track whether the inaccurate detail persists. Focus the work on clearer information and measurable follow-up, without promising a particular recommendation or position.

**Repeat the Review with the Same Questions**

Revisit the same questions after making corrections. Note the new date and preserve the earlier answers so the team can compare the descriptions rather than rely on memory.
Retain errors, omissions and accurate mentions in the record. Selecting only flattering results conceals the variation that the check is meant to reveal. A repeated mismatch warrants closer attention than a single reassuring answer.
Assess the practical result of the work: which information changed, which errors remain and what needs another correction. Clear records support the next audit easier and keep a visibility check from becoming a one-time screenshot exercise.

**Common Questions**

**Can One AI Answer Establish Accuracy?**
It can expose a specific error worth checking, but it cannot represent every answer a customer might receive. Ask the questions again and keep the context of each result.

**Should You Search Only for Your Business Name?**
No. A name-based question tests the known business description. Customer-style questions without the name help you see whether the business appears for relevant needs.

**Does Updating a Website Guarantee an AI Mention?**
No. Clear, accurate information provides readers and search systems better material to work with, but it does not guarantee a mention or a particular answer.

**What to Remember**
Checking AI search accuracy is most helpful as a recurring business-information task. Maintain a reliable set of current facts, ask consistent questions and preserve the wording of the answers. If a detail is wrong, follow the cited material where available and correct the source information you control. Return to those same questions to see what changed. That creates a record of specific issues and completed work, rather than a collection of reassuring screenshots. Prioritize errors that could misdirect customers, and keep expectations realistic about how quickly any external answer may change.
